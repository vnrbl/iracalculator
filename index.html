IRA Calculator FAQ — Retirement, Tax & Distribution Questions

Expert answers on required minimum distributions, Roth IRA vs Traditional IRA, SECURE Act 2.0 rules, catch-up contributions, inherited IRA penalties, and retirement tax planning strategies.

Enacted under the SECURE Act and clarified by SECURE Act 2.0, the 10-year rule requires most non-spouse designated beneficiaries who inherit a traditional or Roth IRA after December 31, 2019, to fully distribute all assets by December 31 of the 10th year following the original owner's death.
It depends on whether the original owner reached their Required Beginning Date (RBD, age 73). Under the IRS final regulations (TD 10001, July 2024):
  • Owner died before RBD: annual distributions in years 1–9 are optional. The entire balance must be zero by December 31 of Year 10.
  • Owner died on or after RBD: you are legally required to take annual RMDs in years 1 through 9 based on your single life expectancy, and liquidate all remaining assets in Year 10.
Under SECURE Act 2.0, the excise penalty for failing to take a required minimum distribution is 25% of the shortfall amount, reduced to 10% if corrected in a timely manner via IRS Form 5329. Transitional penalty relief covered 2021–2024, but full enforcement applies going forward.
Yes — inherited Roth IRAs are subject to the 10-year rule for non-spouse beneficiaries. However, because qualified Roth distributions are tax-free and not subject to annual RMDs, beneficiaries generally do best by leaving the funds invested until Year 10.
Traditional IRA distributions are ordinary taxable income. Leaving a $250,000+ account to compound for a decade can push a single-year distribution into the 32%, 35%, or 37% federal brackets. Smoothing spreads withdrawals across years 1–10 to absorb room in the 12% and 22% brackets, often saving tens of thousands of dollars in total tax.
For 2026, the standard IRA contribution limit is $7,000 per year for individuals under age 50. Those aged 50 and over can make an additional $1,000 catch-up contribution for a total of $8,000. Under SECURE Act 2.0, individuals aged 60 to 63 qualify for an enhanced "super catch-up" contribution. High earners with wages over $150,000 must make catch-up contributions on a Roth (after-tax) basis only.
Your RMD is calculated by dividing your Traditional IRA or inherited IRA account balance as of December 31 of the prior year by the applicable IRS life expectancy factor. For original owners, the Uniform Lifetime Table applies; for inherited IRAs, the Single Life Expectancy Table is used. Under SECURE Act 2.0, RMDs now begin at age 73 (rising to 75 in 2033). Our free retirement calculator automates this using official IRS distribution tables and your specific tax profile.
The optimal choice depends on your current vs. future tax rate. Traditional IRAs provide an upfront tax deduction and tax-deferred growth, making them ideal if you're in a high tax bracket now and expect a lower bracket in retirement. Roth IRAs offer tax-free growth and tax-free withdrawals, which is better if you expect your tax rate to rise or want guaranteed tax-free retirement income. Use our Roth vs Traditional IRA calculator to model both scenarios side-by-side with your actual income and state tax rate.
A common rule of thumb is to have 25 times your annual expenses saved by retirement. However, early retirees at age 55 or 60 face additional challenges: a longer withdrawal horizon, potential early withdrawal penalties before age 59½, and healthcare costs before Medicare eligibility at 65. Our retirement savings calculator helps you project whether your IRA balance, combined with other income sources, will sustain your lifestyle through a 30–40 year retirement.
A backdoor Roth IRA is a legal strategy for high-income earners who exceed Roth IRA income limits. You contribute to a Traditional IRA (non-deductible) and then convert those funds to a Roth IRA. The conversion is taxable on any gains, but if done quickly, the tax impact is minimal. This strategy allows anyone, regardless of income, to benefit from Roth IRA tax-free growth and tax-free retirement withdrawals. Our IRA calculator can model the tax impact of Roth conversions on your retirement projections.

Required Minimum Distributions (RMD) and the SECURE Act 2.0

The Required Minimum Distribution calculator is vital for anyone over age 73 (or inheriting an IRA). Under SECURE Act 2.0, RMDs begin at age 73 and will rise to 75 by 2033. For inherited IRAs, the 10-year distribution rule requires non-spouse beneficiaries to fully liquidate the account within 10 years of the original owner's death. If the owner died after their Required Beginning Date, annual RMDs are mandatory in years 1–9.

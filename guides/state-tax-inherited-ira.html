State-by-State Guide to Inherited IRA Taxation

50-State Review 8 min read Jurisdictional Analysis

While federal tax planning commands the majority of headlines, state income taxes represent a massive, frequently overlooked erosion of inherited wealth. Depending on where the beneficiary resides, state taxes can devour anywhere from 0% to an astonishing 13.3% of an inherited IRA distribution.

Crucial Tax Principle: It does not matter which state the deceased lived in when they funded the IRA. Under federal law (4 U.S.C. Section 114), retirement distributions are taxed based exclusively on the residence of the beneficiary receiving the payment at the time of distribution.

Tier 1: States with No Income Tax on Inherited IRAs (0%)

Beneficiaries residing in the following states pay exactly $0 in state income tax on traditional inherited IRA withdrawals:

  • Alaska
  • Florida
  • Nevada
  • New Hampshire (dividend/interest tax only; no wage or IRA distribution tax)
  • South Dakota
  • Tennessee
  • Texas
  • Washington (capital gains tax only; no ordinary income tax on IRAs)
  • Wyoming

Tier 2: States with Flat Income Tax Rates (Low-to-Medium)

A growing number of states have transitioned to flat personal income tax models, making multi-year bracket modeling simple and predictable:

  • Arizona: Flat 2.5%
  • Colorado: Flat 4.4%
  • Illinois: Flat 4.95% (Note: Illinois specifically exempts most retirement income and IRA distributions from state taxation!)
  • Indiana: Flat 3.05% (plus county surtaxes)
  • North Carolina: Flat 4.5% (phasing down towards 3.99%)
  • Pennsylvania: Flat 3.07% (Pennsylvania generally does not tax retirement distributions from qualified plans if statutory conditions are met)

Tier 3: High-Tax States with Progressive Brackets

Beneficiaries living in high-tax progressive states must be exceptionally vigilant. A massive Year 10 lump sum distribution will not only trigger the highest federal tax tier (37%), but will also catapult the heir into top state tax brackets:

  • California: Top marginal rate of 13.3% (including the 1% Mental Health Services Tax on income over $1M).
  • New York: State top bracket of 10.9%, plus an additional New York City local tax of up to 3.876% for NYC residents.
  • New Jersey: Top marginal rate of 10.75% on taxable income over $1,000,000.
  • Hawaii: Top marginal rate of 11.0%.
  • Minnesota: Top marginal rate of 9.85%.

State Relocation Strategy: Is Moving Worth It?

For beneficiaries inheriting multimillion-dollar traditional accounts, a geographic move can yield extraordinary tax savings.

For instance, distributing a $1,000,000 inherited IRA while residing in California incurs approximately $110,000 to $125,000 in California state income taxes alone. Distributing the exact same account after establishing bona fide legal domicile in Texas, Florida, or Nevada reduces that state liability to $0, saving over six figures in cold cash.

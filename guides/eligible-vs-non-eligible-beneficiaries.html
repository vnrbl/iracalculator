Beneficiary Classifications

Eligible vs. Non-Eligible Designated Beneficiaries

Estate Planning Guide 5 min read SECURE Act Exceptions

While the SECURE Act eliminated the traditional "Stretch IRA" for most Americans, Congress preserved lifetime distribution privileges for a small, protected subset of heirs known as Eligible Designated Beneficiaries (EDBs).

Determining your exact beneficiary classification is the essential first step in evaluating whether you are bound by the strict 10-year countdown or whether you qualify for favorable lifetime distribution schedules.

The 5 Categories of Eligible Designated Beneficiaries (EDBs)

Only five specific categories of individuals qualify as EDBs under the Internal Revenue Code:

1. The Surviving Spouse

Spouses enjoy the greatest flexibility of any beneficiary. A surviving spouse can:

  • Rollover / Treat as Own: Roll the deceased spouse's IRA into their own traditional IRA, deferring RMDs until their own statutory age.
  • Remain as Beneficiary: Elect to remain an inherited IRA beneficiary and stretch distributions over their single life expectancy.
  • Section 327 Election (SECURE 2.0): Elect to be treated as the deceased spouse for RMD timing calculations.

2. A Minor Child of the Deceased Account Owner

Minor biological or legally adopted children of the deceased account owner are permitted to take distributions based on their single life expectancy until they reach the age of majority (age 21 under IRS rules).

The Delayed 10-Year Clock: Once the child reaches age 21, the EDB status expires immediately. The 10-year countdown begins on their 21st birthday, requiring the account to be completely emptied by December 31 of the year the child turns 31.

3. A Disabled Individual

Individuals who meet the statutory definition of permanent disability under IRC Section 72(m)(7)—unable to engage in any substantial gainful activity due to a medically determinable physical or mental impairment—are exempt from the 10-year rule and may stretch distributions over their lifetime.

4. A Chronically Ill Individual

Individuals certified by a licensed physician as unable to perform at least two activities of daily living (eating, bathing, dressing, transferring, etc.) for at least 90 days qualify as chronically ill under IRC Section 7702B(c)(2) and may retain lifetime stretch treatment.

5. An Individual Not More Than 10 Years Younger than the Deceased

Beneficiaries who are older than the deceased account owner, or who are younger by 10 years or less (such as siblings, cousins, or domestic partners of similar age), qualify as EDBs and can take annual distributions based on their single life expectancy.

Who is a Non-Eligible Designated Beneficiary (NEDB)?

Any individual named directly on the beneficiary designation form who does not satisfy one of the five EDB criteria is an NEDB.

This includes adult, healthy children, grandchildren, nieces, nephews, and friends. NEDBs are subject to the mandatory 10-year rule and should use tax-bracket smoothing to prevent excessive tax penalties.
